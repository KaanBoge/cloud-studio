Verification report · 7 September 2026

The historical simulations are not yet a validated matched-code comparison. The fixes below do not retroactively correct existing simulations. Published meshes and MP4s are visualizations, not complete raw-data backups.

Verified and corrected

Eight native grid codes passed 24 initial-output tests: Athena++, AthenaPK, Athena 4.2, FLASH 4.8, Flash-X, Enzo, Enzo-E and RAMSES, each at χ = 10, 100 and 1000 on a 64 × 32 × 32 grid. Tests checked density, velocity, pressure, domain and cell count. The four particle-code IC generators passed 12 separate input-field tests; their evolved solver results were not validated here.

The corrected prescription has zero velocity inside 1.3 cloud radii and constant wind velocity outside, with the existing tanh density edge. Grønnow et al. (2018), section 2 specifies a sharp velocity boundary, not a tanh velocity law. This is not an exact reproduction of its entire setup or of Braspenning et al.

Analysis now reads physical parameters from input files, handles FLASH's radius and AthenaPK's units, and normalizes dense mass to its fixed initial value. The rectangular RAMSES reader now includes all native cells. Historical mass curves are withheld from the viewer until corrected native-data diagnostics exist.

The destructive Athena++ rerun path was replaced, and the raw-data cleanup daemon was disabled. The new runner preserves raw outputs and uses distinct versioned run IDs.

Measured performance improvements

Performance update: Short matched-setting tests on this PC reduced Athena++ wall time by 15% at level 5 and 18% at level 6; AthenaPK level 5 took 30% less time. Larger GPU blocks also reduced sampled level-6 GPU memory use from roughly 15.3 to 12.8 GiB. Full-field test comparisons passed, including the native tracers. These are short tests, not full production timings or new completed comparison runs. Measured evidence, optimized launch scripts and reuse guidance.

Second performance pass: The current profiles now use a separately validated LTO build at CPU level 5 (6.2% less time in repeated tests) and faster lossless snapshot encoding at GPU level 6 (12.4% less time in repeated output-including tests). Decoded GPU fields matched exactly. The CPU level-6 candidate was slower and was rejected; its previous settings remain. These improvements are not universal or full-run accuracy claims. Second-pass evidence and decisions.

Not yet established

Equal boundary conditions, passive tracers and effective particle resolution across every code; complete corrected production runs at every level; complete measured frame cadence for all historical runs; validated cooling and Galilean-frame variants. These remain necessary before drawing a matched-code scientific conclusion.
