Verification log

Checks and corrections to the simulation setup, the analysis and the run tools, up to 7 September 2026.

Starting conditions

Grid codes. Eight grid codes passed 24 tests of their first saved state: Athena++, AthenaPK, Athena 4.2, FLASH 4.8, Flash-X, Enzo, Enzo-E and RAMSES, each at χ 10, 100 and 1000 on a 64 × 32 × 32 grid. The tests checked density, velocity, pressure, domain and cell count.

Particle codes. The generators of the starting particles of the four particle codes passed 12 separate tests of their input fields.

Sharp velocity edge. The corrected setup has zero velocity inside 1.3 cloud radii and the full wind velocity outside, and keeps the smooth tanh density edge. This follows the sharp velocity boundary described in Grønnow et al. (2018), section 2.

Analysis and run tools

Analysis. The analysis reads the physical parameters from the input files, handles the FLASH cloud radius and the AthenaPK units, and divides the dense mass by its measured starting value. The RAMSES reader includes every cell of the rectangular box. The viewer holds back the mass curves of the earlier runs until there are diagnostics from corrected data.

Run tools. The Athena++ rerun path that removed raw data was replaced, and the raw data cleanup job was switched off. The new runner keeps every raw output and gives each run its own versioned name. No native output was deleted during these repairs.

Follow up repairs. The analysis checks physical metadata against the native inputs of each run, the launchers check the free space of the Windows drive behind WSL, and two older run scripts that deleted data were retired. 42 Python tests and two viewer JavaScript checks pass. Report, scripts, logs and test evidence

Viewer. The viewer keeps measured times beyond t = 5 tcc, labels Mach variants separately, matches its time labels to the mesh on screen while downloads are pending, and shows the measured time of each comparison pane. A browser test confirmed the loading behaviour and a streamed final frame without errors.

Faster runs

First pass. Short tests with matched settings cut the Athena++ wall time by 15 percent at level 5 and by 18 percent at level 6, and AthenaPK took 30 percent less time at level 5. Larger GPU blocks lowered the sampled level 6 GPU memory from about 15.3 to 12.8 GiB. Full field comparisons, tracers included, matched. Measurements, launch scripts and reuse guidance

Second pass. A separately tested LTO build saves 6.2 percent of the time of CPU runs at level 5 in repeated tests, and faster lossless snapshot encoding saves 12.4 percent of the time of GPU runs at level 6 in repeated tests with output. The decoded GPU fields matched exactly. A CPU candidate at level 6 was slower, so the earlier settings stayed. Measurements and decisions

Tracking and cooling

Tracking tests. With the conditions unchanged, late restarts at all three contrasts, checks of the MPI rank count and checks of the frame displacement at every step pass. Three level 4 tests each reached 5 tcc with 101 native snapshots; at χ 100 and χ 1000 they keep 98.4 and 95.4 percent of their starting tracer mass. Measured results and data locations

Tracking prototype. A separate Athena++ build passed tests with tracking off and with restarts. Six level 3 tests reached 5 tcc with 101 native outputs each, and the tracked χ 1000 test kept 88.9 percent of its starting tracer mass in the box.

Cooling. The nine cooling inputs of that time did not switch cooling on, and their native logs report the cooling parameters as unused, so those runs are non radiative. The viewer labels them that way, and the old cooling generator no longer writes such an input.
